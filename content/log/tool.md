If you have been working on the student farm, you are not a stranger to many different tools like a shovel or a harvest knife. You probably have used a pair of scissors or pruners to chop something during harvest too. They are usually a little funky, not as sharp as you would expect, and people would just briefly dip them into the bleach solution after they are done using them. That is completely normal (is it?) in a harvest setting. However, once you step into the world of pruning, your pruning tools are like swords to a swordsman (or woman, or human beings). Keeping them clean, sharp and well-maintained is your life mission, so when you are fighting (against the branches), you are always ready.

## Types

There are so many different types of pruning tools you can use, in MG we have limited options. I will introduce their names.

### Pruner/Clipper/Trimmer

Or if you want, you can call them gardening scissors, These are just scissor like structures you can prune thinner branches, usually under 2 in. There are many different types, but the basic mechanism is the same, usually involving some blades, handles and a spring.

### Loppers

I cannot spell this and I put lobster first. They are pretty much just a bigger version of pruners. You can cut higher (perfect for short queens like me) and thicker branches with them. They are usually harder to maintain, since it is relatively harder to take every part off and clean them individually. People also love to put them upside down into the ground (if I saw you do this I would scream). Please, think of the loppers as a kid and the tip as the head, it is already bad enough to just put your kid on the ground (if you are a sim I won’t say much), don’t land them with their heads on the ground.

### Hand Saws

My favorite tools so far. Requires some technique and strength, more dangerous but now you can cut off a whole scaffold, wow! Unfortunately, on Market Garden we only have those foldable ones but no pole saws. They are still very handy. Be aware of how quickly these blades can be worn off due to inappropriate use, and the replacement of the blade is very expensive (or sharpen them if you can, it is very time consuming).

## Maintenance

Our honored graduated LSF Alejandro complained about how all the tools are just so rusty before the pruning season starts. Most of the pruners are so rusty and crappy. To make sure we don’t have to buy new tools every year, maintenance, use and storage is important. I will have a separate article on usage, so let’s just focus on maintenance and storage here.

Although the blades are usually iron, other parts, such as the junction and the spring, are usually iron. If you find a really rusty pruner, carefully take off all the parts and scrub off the rust with a piece of steel wool. Always have another same pair of pruners by your side so you know how to put everything back together. You can oil the connection at the same time. More demonstrations can be found either online or you can come for demonstration and practice when we are doing that.

It is essential to sanitize them every time, and water can build up during that process. Try to dry the excess water after your sanitation (which you should always do, because you don’t want to leave a wet wound out there) and make sure your tools are dried before storage. Put the tools and the box in a relatively dry place inside (they usually go back to the shop after a day). Maintaining and sharpening your tool is also helpful for you to make a clean cut that would allow the tree to heal better.

## Sanitation

PLEASE, SANITIZE, YOUR, TOOL. Either with bleach, lysol or alcohol. I personally prefer alcohol since it dries faster. Sanitize your tool after you are done with one tree, you dropped your tool, or you cut down a disease infected tissue. If you are a little concerned, such as if it touches your clothes (yes that can also spread the disease), just sanitize it. Be aware of any tiny material that could contaminate your tools: soil, plant, water, and yourself.
